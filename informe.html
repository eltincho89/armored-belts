What it is

A side-branch, not a higher tier

With Space Age installed, the turbo belt already sits above express. Making armored faster would have obsoleted a whole expansion tier. Instead it keeps the exact speed of express and changes everything else.

Retrofits without tearing down Keeps fast_replaceable_group, so it drops right on top of an existing line. And next_upgrade is deliberately left empty, so no upgrade planner moves it afterward.

Recipes use the plain crafting category, no lubricant: hand-craftable. Repairing the line mid-attack shouldn't require a trip back to the chemical plant. An armored belt costs 1 express belt + 4 steel; the unlock requires logistics-3 and military-3.

Findings

Two oddities in the vanilla data

Read directly from the installed game's prototypes. Neither is common knowledge among most players.

Upgrading your belts makes them more flammable

The yellow belt resists fire at 90%. The moment you move to red it falls to 50%, and stays there forever: blue and turbo are still at 50. So the game's normal progression makes your fire resistance worse while you think you're upgrading.

150 HP / 90% → 160 HP / 50% → 170 HP / 50% → 170 HP / 50%

No belt has explosion resistance

Not at any tier. The field simply doesn't exist in the vanilla prototypes. Any explosive damage —your own artillery, rockets, chain explosions— hits your belts at 100%.

The twist: biters deal no fire or explosion damage Pulling the enemies' actual offensive damage shows only two types: physical (bite) and acid (spitter and worm spit). Zero fire, zero explosion. So fire and explosion don't protect you from biters — they protect you from your own friendly fire: flamethrower turrets torching your line, artillery, tree fires. That's why the mod ended up with all four resistances instead of the two the original idea called for.

Impact

How much it actually survives

The flat 8-point reduction on physical damage means small biters can't damage it at all. Since the technology requires production and military science, by the time you have it small biters are no longer a threat — so in practice this breaks nothing. Dropping that value to 4 would bring them back into play if more tension is wanted.

Against behemoths, the number that actually matters, it holds at ~10×.

Art

From cyan to steel without losing the relief

The first attempt used Factorio's tint field. It didn't work, and the reason is interesting.

tint multiplies over the base sprite, and multiplying can only darken channels, never lift them. Starting from a saturated cyan —low R, high B— no tint value produces neutral gray. It kept reading as blueish no matter what.

Analyzing the sprites revealed the shortcut: they're already mostly metallic gray. Between 44% and 59% of opaque pixels have saturation below 0.1. What makes them blue is a cyan accent on top of dark metal. So there was no need to push anything to grayscale —that would have flattened the tread's relief and killed the sense of motion—: it was enough to strip the chroma and keep luminance intact.

Impossible to reference a sprite that doesn't exist The recolor script writes the Lua path map from the files it actually produced. The mod remaps against that map: a path that isn't listed is left untouched, and one that is is guaranteed to exist on disk. Adding a sprite to the script is enough for the Lua to pick it up on its own.

Quality control

Three bugs that didn't break the load

None of the three produced an error. All three would have reached the player.

  1. 01

    Upgrade chain inherited from Space Age

    The clone carried over next_upgrade = "turbo-transport-belt". It would have pulled armored belts into the automatic upgrade toward turbo — exactly the opposite of what was intended.

  2. 02

    Blue wreckage on destruction

    Corpse prototypes carry their own icon, independent of the entity's. Without fixing it, a destroyed armored belt left wreckage with the express's cyan icon.

  3. 03

    Dangling reference after a refactor

    Rewriting the shared module removed the tint constant, but the technology still referenced it. In Lua that doesn't fail: it evaluates to nil. The research icon stayed untinted blue and the validator passed without a complaint.

The third is the entire argument for verifying the result and not just the absence of errors.

Method

How it was verified

Factorio supports --dump-data: it boots, processes every prototype from every active mod, writes a JSON dump, and exits. It's the real data-stage check without opening a save.

Against that 29 MB dump, the following was checked programmatically: final health, speed and resistance values for the three entities; ingredients and results of the three recipes; prerequisites and cost of the technology; that the 17 referenced sprites exist on disk; and that not a single graphical reference to express survives across the six entities and corpses.

Result: exit 0, zero errors, loading alongside Space Age and 30 other mods.

TIL upgrading your belts makes them MORE flammable — so I made a mod

I was digging through the game's prototypes while building an armored belt mod and found this:

  yellow belt   150 HP   fire 90%
  red belt      160 HP   fire 50%   <- drops
  blue belt     170 HP   fire 50%
  turbo belt    170 HP   fire 50%

The basic yellow belt has 90% fire resistance. The moment you upgrade to red it falls to 50% and stays there forever. The normal progression of the game makes your fire resistance worse while you think you're upgrading.

There's more: no belt in the game has any explosion resistance at all. Not one tier. The field simply doesn't exist in the vanilla prototypes.

The second finding changed my mod's design. I pulled the actual offensive damage types off the enemies and there are only two: physical (biter melee) and acid (spitter and worm spit). No fire, no explosion. Which means fire resistance doesn't protect you from biters — it protects you from YOUR OWN flamethrower turrets torching your belt line, your own artillery, and tree fires. Which is what actually eats a frontline belt in practice.

So the mod ended up with all four resistances:

  Armored Transport Belts
  - Same speed as express (0.09375) — not a speed tier
  - 600 HP per segment instead of 170
  - Fire 95%, explosion 80%, physical 60%, acid 60%
  - Hand-craftable: 1 express belt + 4 steel
  - Unlocks after logistics-3 and military-3

It survives roughly 10x longer than express against behemoth biters, 19x against medium ones.

Two design calls I think matter more than the numbers:

It's a side-grade, not a higher tier. With Space Age, turbo belts already sit above express — making armored belts faster would have obsoleted a whole expansion tier. They keep express speed and only change survivability. You trade throughput for armor, you don't get both.

It keeps fast-replace and has no upgrade target. You can lay armored belts straight over an existing frontline without tearing it down, and no upgrade planner will move them afterward.

Before starting I checked the full mod portal index (22,942 mods). There are mods that make belts invincible with a toggle, and mods that add speed tiers, but none that treat the belt as defensive infrastructure. If I missed one, tell me.

Balance feedback very welcome. My main doubt: the flat physical damage reduction makes small biters unable to hurt it at all. The tech is mid-to-late game so I think it's fine in practice, but I'll admit it sounds strong.
